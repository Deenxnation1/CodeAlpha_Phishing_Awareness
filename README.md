## 📌 Overview

This project is a security awareness training presentation designed to educate everyday users and employees on how phishing attacks work, how attackers manipulate human psychology, how to recognize phishing red flags, and how to respond safely[cite: 2, 4]. It includes a clickable interactive quiz slide flow with feedback for both correct and incorrect choices[cite: 4].

---

## 📂 Presentation Structure

The presentation (`Phishing Awareness Training — Cyber Security Best Practices.pptx`) covers the following core sections[cite: 4]:

1. **Title & Intern Branding:** Introduces the training topic, intern details (**Shamsudeen Ibrahim Danmusa**), and **CodeAlpha** internship branding[cite: 1, 4].
2. **What is Phishing?:** Explains how attackers impersonate trusted sources—such as banks, streaming services, or workplace leadership—to steal sensitive information[cite: 4].
3. **Common Social Engineering Tactics:** Covers psychological triggers attackers use to rush or deceive victims[cite: 2, 4]:
   - **Urgency / Fear:** Claims like *"Your account will be suspended in 24 hours!"*[cite: 4]
   - **Greed / Reward:** Lures such as *"You won a $500 Amazon Gift Card!"*[cite: 4]
   - **Authority:** Impersonation such as *"This is the CEO, buy gift cards for our clients right now."*[cite: 4]
4. **How to Spot Phishing (Red Flags):** Highlights practical warning signs[cite: 2, 4]:
   - **Suspicious Sender Email:** Lookalike domains such as `support@paypa1-security.com` instead of `@paypal.com`[cite: 4].
   - **Generic Greetings:** Impersonal openings like `"Dear Customer"` instead of a real name[cite: 4].
   - **Fake Links:** Display text that looks legitimate (such as `www.bank.com`) while pointing elsewhere (such as `http://login-verify-user.ru`)[cite: 4].
   - **Spelling & Grammar Errors:** Typos or awkward phrasing[cite: 4].
5. **Real-World Examples (Visual Screenshots):** Outlines side-by-side comparisons of legitimate versus phishing emails and marking red flags with visual callouts[cite: 4].
6. **Best Practices & Action Steps:** Recommends avoiding unverified links, verifying requests via official channels or saved bookmarks, and enabling Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA/2FA) across accounts[cite: 4].
7. **Interactive Quiz ("Spot the Phishing Email!"):** Presents a sample university/college email screenshot asking whether it is legitimate or a scam and why, with dedicated outcome slides for **Option A (Legitimate)** and **Option B (Scam/Phishing)** and **Back to Question** navigation[cite: 4].

## 🧪 Quiz Case Study Summary

In the interactive quiz, learners evaluate an email referencing a student market and open days with a **"View message"** button[cite: 4]:

- **Option A (Legitimate — Incorrect):** Explains why the university context can look convincing at a glance, then points out the mismatched college domains (`@bilborough.ac.uk` vs. `cardiffmet.ac.uk`), the hidden message behind the **"View message"** button, and conflicting details between the subject line and body text[cite: 4].
- **Option B (Scam / Phishing — Correct):** Confirms the phishing/compromised-account red flags—sender/context mismatch, generic **"View message"** button risk, inconsistent topic/formatting—and advises verifying through official channels or reporting the message[cite: 4].
